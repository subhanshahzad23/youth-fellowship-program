Goodcase Website Redesign 1. Purpose of this request Goodcase is looking for a design agency or freelancer to set our visual direction, refresh our brand and design a new goodcase.io, live by 31 December 2026. We want a partner who does more than draw screens. We expect you to guide us on how the brand should look and feel, then carry that direction through a complete website design. 2. About Goodcase Goodcase is the back office of U.S. immigration law firms: we prepare petitions and manage cases for attorneys, who keep the strategy, the client and the signature. We are not a law firm and do not give legal advice. ● What we do. Two services. Case drafting : filing-ready O-1, EB-1A, EB-2 NIW, H-1B, L-1 and E-2 petition packages, drafted from the attorney's strategy and in their format. Case management : an experienced case manager working inside the client's firm, supported by Casey, case management software we built. ● Who we serve. U.S. immigration law firms of every size, from solo attorneys to high-volume practices. ● Our team. Around 30 business immigration professionals across Europe, Latin America and India, with more than 1,000 cases worked for U.S. law firms. ● How we sell. Flat fees per case, starting at $500, no minimum volume, first case at half price and a money-back guarantee. Our buyers are U.S. immigration attorneys. They are detail-oriented, risk-averse and judge a vendor by whether it looks trustworthy enough to touch their clients' files. Most of them have never heard of us before they land on the site. The current site is at goodcase.io . It no longer reflects how we work or who we serve, which is why we are rebuilding it.

## Page 2

3. Goals and visual direction The site must look as professional as the premium legal brands our clients know, while feeling approachable enough for a solo attorney to book a call, because we are the more affordable alternative. That tension is the core of the brief. We want: ● Professional and clean. Quiet, confident, legal in tone. Generous white space, strong typography, restrained color. Nothing that looks like a startup template or a stock-photo agency. ● Accessible, not exclusive. Premium legal tech brands often signal "enterprise only". We want the same credibility with a warmer, more human feel: real people, plain language, prices shown openly. ● Human behind every process. Our main message is that a specialized team stands behind every petition. The visuals should support that, not hide it behind abstract graphics. ● Built to convert. Every page leads to one action: book a 30-minute call. The first-case offer appears under every hero and above every closing button. References we like for their clarity and polish: harvey.ai , legora.com and manifestlaw.com . We do not want a copy of any of them. Tell us what you would take from them and what you would change for a brand at our price point. We are open to a refreshed logo, palette and typography. We see this as a small rebrand, not a new name. 4. Scope of work The core scope is brand direction plus the full website design; development is a separately priced option so we can compare design fees fairly. A. Brand and visual direction (core) ● Two or three visual directions (mood boards or style tiles) with a recommendation and your reasoning ● Color palette, typography and spacing rules ● Direction for photography, team portraits, icons and any illustration ● A short brand guide our team can use for social media, proposals and presentations

## Page 3

B. Website design (core) Desktop and mobile designs for the pages in our copy document. We expect roughly 20 page designs: Group Pages Main Home, Case drafting, Case management, About (incl. our process and security), Book a call Who we serve Overview, Solo and small firms, Growing firms, Large and high-volume Resources Hub, Blog list and article, Podcast list and episode, Case studies list and detail, Sample work list and detail, FAQ Other Casey (a short page handing off to Casey's own site), AI Use Policy, Privacy Policy The design must include these components: header with two dropdown menus, footer with newsletter signup, a fact strip, service cards, a four-step process, an ROI calculator, video testimonials, a client logo row, a booking calendar embed and a contact form. Deliver a reusable design system (components and styles) in Figma, so we can build new pages without coming back to you each time. C. Development (optional) Recommend a platform (for example Webflow, Framer or WordPress) and explain why. Our team must be able to publish blog posts, podcast episodes and case studies without a developer. Include basic SEO setup (titles and descriptions are in the copy document), analytics, redirects from the current site, and migration of existing content (blog posts, sample work, podcast episodes, FAQ, team) with categories and tags. Out of scope: copywriting (our copy is final, though we welcome your comments on layout and length), the Casey product site, and paid advertising.

## Page 6

7. What your proposal should include Keep it short: a proposal of five to ten pages that answers the points below is better than a long agency deck. 1. Price. A fixed price for part A (brand) and part B (website design), shown separately and net of VAT, in EUR or PLN. If you offer development (part C), price it as its own line. State what is included: number of directions, revision rounds, page count. List any extras and their rates. 2. Experience with similar projects. Two to four projects similar to ours: B2B professional services, legal, legal tech or a brand refresh with a website. Links to live sites, plus your role in each. 3. Approach. How you would set the visual direction for a brand that must look premium but feel accessible. A paragraph on what you would take from our reference sites and what you would avoid. 4. Team. Who will do the work, their experience, and who our day-to-day contact is. 5. Timeline. A week-by-week plan that meets our dates, with review points and what you need from us at each. 6. Communication. How and how often you will update us, your working language and hours (our team works in CET; English is required), and the tools you use. 7. Payment terms and one reference from a recent client.

The lowest complete proposal scores 70 points; others score in proportion (lowest price ÷ your price × 70). Part C is compared separately. Experience with similar projects 20% Quality and relevance of the work you show: professional services, legal or legal tech, brand refreshes with websites. Communication and timelines 10% A realistic plan that meets our dates, clear review points, responsiveness during this process. Proposals that leave out a required price, or cannot meet the 31 December 2026 hard deadline, will not be scored. We may hold a short call with up to three shortlisted bidders before deciding. We are not obliged to accept the lowest or any proposal.
